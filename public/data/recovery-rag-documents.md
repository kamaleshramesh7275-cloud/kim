# Sports Recovery Knowledge Base

## Recovery Basics

Recovery after training depends on sleep, hydration, nutrition, and managing fatigue. Athletes usually recover best when they keep a steady sleep schedule, drink enough fluids, eat enough carbohydrates and protein, and avoid pushing through severe soreness or warning signs.

## Hydration Guidance

Hydration should be ongoing rather than front-loaded. Athletes should drink regularly through the day, add electrolytes during heavy sweating, and monitor urine color and thirst as simple indicators of hydration status.

## Sleep and Fatigue

Low sleep lowers recovery, raises fatigue, and makes injuries and poor performance more likely. If sleep is poor, the safest response is to reduce training intensity, prioritize rest, and keep the next day lighter until recovery improves.

## Nutrition for Recovery

A good recovery meal usually includes protein for muscle repair, carbohydrates for energy restoration, and fluids to replace sweat losses. Post-training meals and snacks should be practical, balanced, and easy to digest.

## Injury and Load Management

If soreness, pain, or fatigue is rising, training load should be reduced. Athletes recovering from injury should follow a structured plan, avoid high-impact loading too early, and increase intensity gradually only when symptoms are improving.

## Coaching Advice

Coaches should treat recovery as a daily decision. When fatigue, soreness, or stress rises, the best response is to reduce volume, protect sleep, and focus on quality rather than pushing through discomfort.
